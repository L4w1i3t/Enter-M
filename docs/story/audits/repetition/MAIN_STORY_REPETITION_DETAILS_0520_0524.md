Status labels:

- **NEW** — this exact recurrence or aggregate habit is not specifically identified in the existing notes.
- **NEW — extends annotated pattern** — individual instances may be annotated, but the lexical/syntactic cluster is not.
- **ALREADY ANNOTATED** — a local note or the revision index already identifies substantially the same problem.
- **KEEP / FALSE POSITIVE** — repetition is intentional, character-bearing, or structurally useful; trim explanations around it, not the repetition itself.

Priority:

- **P1** — conspicuous repetition, continuity-visible duplication, or a high-yield revision target.
- **P2** — meaningful prose/voice/pacing improvement.
- **P3** — sentence-level polish; address after the larger cuts.

## Executive findings

1. **May 24’s “enough” habit is the clearest unannotated word-level problem.** It appears 36 times in story-facing text, with eleven uses between `0524.rpy:104` and `0524.rpy:240`. It repeatedly measures how perfectly calibrated a sound, smile, shift, glance, bow, compliment, or silence is. This makes the narrator sound as socially optimized as Sam and often performs the interpretation before the action can breathe. **NEW, P1.**
2. **Several lines are reused almost verbatim across adjacent days.** The most visible are “The warning bell rings below us, thin and distant” (`0523.rpy:412-413`, `0524.rpy:642-643`), Himeko arriving home late enough that MC “only” hears her downstairs (`0520.rpy:521-522`, `0521.rpy:803-805`), and Monika “look[ing] at me for a second, the way…” (`0522.rpy:411`, `0524.rpy:777`; compare `0521.rpy:650-651`). **NEW, P1.**
3. **The five days share one calibration vocabulary:** *just, still, enough, exactly, apparently, actually, probably, honestly, small, quiet,* and *normal*. These words often soften or pre-interpret observations rather than add information. The problem peaks in May 23–24. **NEW — extends annotated pattern, P1/P2.**
4. **Legal/administrative humor remains heavily saturated.** Court, counsel, files, evidence, verdicts, sentences, jurisdiction, procedure, bureaucracy, contracts, prosecution, and paperwork recur in every setting: home, class, track, club, romance, games, and transitions. Himeko and MC legitimately own this register, but it spreads to Monika, Matsuda, Asahina, teachers, Coach, and the narrator. **ALREADY ANNOTATED, P1.**
5. **Tsubaki’s social anxiety is repeatedly rendered through the same choreography:** half-second relief, tightened hands, a backward/caught step, delayed smile, lowered gaze, apology, then narration translating the gesture. The individual scenes are strong; the recurrence makes her transparent and predictable. **ALREADY ANNOTATED, with new cross-file exact catches, P1.**
6. **The prose repeatedly timestamps emotional beats with “for a second,” “for half a second,” “a beat,” “for a while,” and “before X can…”.** This produces a metronomic, stage-directed cadence. May 24 alone uses “before” 32 times; May 23 uses it 29 times. **NEW, P2.**
7. **Objects and abstractions are personified at very high frequency.** Rooms remember, hold, breathe, loosen, and decide; days behave and hide things; sentences breathe, deserve help, or run into traffic; phones demand attention; homework has leverage; the future takes notes or becomes a shape. Individual images are witty, but their ubiquity makes every scene share the same authored intelligence. **NEW — extends annotated metaphor notes, P2.**
8. **School, rooftop, club, track, phone, and day-ending transitions reuse the same templates.** “By the final bell,” rooftop-door entrances, warning bells, “afternoon classes take [the moment] away,” facedown-phone gestures, and Nier recaps recur with little variation. **ALREADY ANNOTATED in broad pacing terms; exact repeats are NEW, P1/P2.**
9. **The poems remain the correct keeper.** The repeated bus/window/cup/table/pressure images are not themselves the central problem. The surplus lies in prompts, pre-poem paraphrase, post-poem explanation, character-thought recaps, and repeated scene landings. **KEEP / FALSE POSITIVE.**
