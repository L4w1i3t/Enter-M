- **ALREADY ANNOTATED**: a nearby `REVIEW`/`REVISION` note or the annotation index explicitly identifies substantially the same problem.
- **NEW**: not explicitly identified in the existing notes.
- **NEW SYNTHESIS**: local symptoms may be annotated, but the cross-file pattern and its cumulative effect are not.

Priority:

- **P1**: major fatigue or repeated thesis; address in the first prose cut.
- **P2**: meaningful sentence/scene-level improvement.
- **P3**: minute polish, dosage watch, or low-cost variation.

Line counts and lexical counts below refer to the current files. A displayed-string scan (comments excluded; dialogue, narration, menus, and phone text included) found the following unusually dense words across this five-file span: **`enough` in 68 strings / 71 total occurrences**, **`just` 67 / 67**, **`still` 59 / 62**, **`small`/`little` 46 / 46**, **`exact`/`exactly` 34 / 35**, **`clean` 25 / 26**, **`shape` 14 / 14**, and **`room` 74 / 76**. Counts are diagnostic, not automatic deletion orders.

## Highest-value conclusions

1. **May 25 is not merely long; it repeats its method.** It repeatedly uses a concrete action, an interpretive contrast (`not X, just Y`), an intensity calibrator (`enough`, `small`, `exactly`), a controlled silence, and a recap. Cutting only the ten already inventoried “landings” will help, but the sentence-level tics also need variation.
2. **Exam repetition has three different layers and should not be treated as one problem.**
   - The recurring exam-day structure is useful ritual.
   - The question/checkpoint machinery is mechanically necessary.
   - The three-part response prose (selected answer, explanation, italic answer/mnemonic) is avoidable and creates most of the quiz fatigue.
3. **The caught routes repeat one disciplinary grammar.** Across subjects, an adult becomes very quiet, names a zero, converts the subject into a moral metaphor, and the protagonist imagines Mom/Monika/records before a long guilt coda. A player sees at most two subject catches before the bad end, but any pairing still feels authored from the same template.
4. **The two Himeko confrontations are strong but structurally duplicate one another.** Both begin with the front door, phone, standing mother, bag command, rejected explanation, choice-versus-panic distinction, institutional inventory, obedience/remorse lesson, and stairs coda. The repeat confrontation should begin later and contain only what changes: trust becoming identity doubt, maternal inability, and love coexisting with distrust.
5. **Abstract motif words are overburdened.** `shape`, `clean`, `room`, `enough`, and `exactly` repeatedly tell the player what degree or category an emotion occupies. The words are individually in voice; collectively they make unrelated speakers and scenes feel curated by the same narrator.
