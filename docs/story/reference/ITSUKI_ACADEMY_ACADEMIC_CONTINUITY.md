## School baseline

- Fundamentals may appear as warmups, prerequisites, or sources of mistakes.
- Assessed work should normally ask students to use those fundamentals rather

- A difficult prompt should remain readable to a player who has not taken the

  unexplained jargon or arbitrarily obscure facts.

## Grade-level ladder

### First-year work

First-year material establishes disciplinary foundations and asks for controlled
application. Appropriate work includes:

- identifying variables and controls inside a concrete experimental setup;
- explaining diffusion, osmosis, cellular energy, photosynthesis, and
  respiration with correct relationships;
- interpreting a modest graph, diagram, or short data table;
- applying a familiar model to a new example;
- distinguishing closely related terms and explaining why the distinction
  matters.

At an above-average school, first-year work should not make Asahina look as if
she is only naming pictures or repeating junior-high facts. She can understand
the ideas while still becoming stuck, overlooking a control, losing confidence,
or needing a second explanation.

### Senior work

Senior material assumes the foundations and places the difficulty in transfer,
synthesis, and limits of inference. Appropriate work includes:

- applying constitutional principles to a case where several doctrines are
  relevant;
- comparing textual patterns and defending an interpretation with evidence;
- using experimental results to narrow a biological mechanism without claiming
  more than the data proves;
- interpreting linkage data, ecological change, equilibrium, reaction yield,
  or measurement limits;
- carrying algebra through factoring, domain restrictions, extraneous
  solutions, inverses, and interpretation of a model;
- distinguishing grammatical forms that are both possible but carry different
  emphasis, and qualifying a reading claim to match the evidence.

The basics can remain visible inside those tasks. They should be tools MC uses,
not the intellectual destination of his senior review.

## Player-facing question design

- Use plausible misconceptions as distractors. Do not make the wrong answers
  jokes unless the joke belongs to a later character reaction.
- Make the correct choice the best-supported conclusion, especially when the
  evidence cannot prove a single cause.
- Let a wrong branch reveal the point of failure, then return MC to the shared
  conclusion without humiliating him.
- Keep the problem statement and its decisive evidence on the page. Outside
  knowledge may enrich the answer, but it should not be required to play.
- Preserve character business around the material: gestures, teasing, fatigue,
  pauses, practical care, and imperfect explanations keep study prose alive.
- Do not make classroom passages or word problems annotate the main plot so
  precisely that they become an authorial answer key.
