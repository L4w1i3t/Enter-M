# Main-story prose and authorial-intent guide

Date established: 2026-08-31

Last updated: 2026-09-25 (source/links reviewed; author direction retained)

Scope: the written main ENTER M story. The current scripts remain working prose,
including revisions to `game/story/season1/0515.rpy` through `0531.rpy`.

This guide combines direct author instructions with past editorial analysis.
Documentation is fallible: an agent calling an edit accepted, authoritative,
or validated does not establish author approval or prove that the prose works.
The author explicitly clarified this on September 5. Use independent judgment
against their hard rules and the actual story, including when correcting a
previously documented revision.

1. The author's explicit instructions and corrections, including the current
   request. Preserve intentional characterization, ambiguity, and story scope.
2. Live story evidence and route/state behavior. These establish what has
   happened, while still allowing continuity errors to be repaired.
3. This guide's direct author instructions. Editorial interpretations remain
   subject to the first two sources.
4. `reference/ITSUKI_ACADEMY_ACADEMIC_CONTINUITY.md` for the school,
   grade-level, course-rigor, and player-facing assessment standard.
5. Revision logs and intent-bearing source comments as history to investigate,
   not restrictions on an authorized new revision.
6. `../history/story-revisions.md` summarizes superseded audits and reviews.
   Their diagnoses and opinions require fresh verification against the prose.
7. `../project/roadmap.md` (built from `../GOALS.txt` on September 21) for
   long-range planning. Unwritten events and unresolved future beats remain
   provisional. `../history/early-story-design.md` preserves older background.

The September 4 pilot's punctuation targets, sentence-length rankings,
mandatory restarts, and character-wide "never" prescriptions do not govern
later work. In particular, no ellipsis quota applies to MC, Tsubaki need not
restart every statement, and longer sentences do not by themselves mean faster

## Direct story intent

- The theme is tenacity, or perseverance (author, September 21): life may
  knock you down and kick you while you are down, and you get up, dust
  yourself off, and keep going. Himeko states it in her own way on May 23,
  while she and MC look at colleges ("Walk instead if you need to").
- Act 1 presents itself as school-life slice of life while functioning as a
  dark comedy with a continuous darker narrative under the daily episodes.
- The ordinary days are real story material. School, clubs, meals, errands,
  calls, games, injuries, and household labor create the time whose later loss
  must matter.
- Darkness exists to engage recognizable teenage and young-adult pressures,
  not to supply spectacle for its own sake.
- The tonal change should begin as deniable irregularity. Early disturbances
  create a quiet "wait, what?" response before the underlying narrative forces
  itself into the foreground.
- Later Act 1 should become winterlike: quiet, monochrome, subdued, and bleak,
  followed by a bittersweet sense of spring rather than a clean reset.
- Chapter 5 (January 1 to March 7) should feel somber, isolating, and almost
  frozen in place, not psychologically horrifying or intense like the chapters
  before it (author, September 21). MC is at his lowest and loneliest, and
  Kotonoha is the one hand that still reaches out. See the Chapter 5 section
  of `../project/roadmap.md`.
- The trailer's locked atmospheric vocabulary is "mystique, not angelic" and
  "ethereal, not cosmic": cool hollow color, broadcast decay, a signal that can
  barely hold, and things slightly wrong. Tease; do not reveal.

### Friendship progression (September 7 author direction)

The six-person group of MC, Asahina, Sam, Tsubaki, Monika, and Matsuda should
be well-defined by the end of June. May can create warmth and a desire for
another encounter while the newer connections are still developing.

Preserve the unequal starting relationships: MC and Asahina are siblings,
MC and Monika already have a year of friendship, Asahina and Sam are dating,
and Matsuda and Tsubaki have their own friendship. Their ease does not
establish equivalent familiarity between the other pairs.

Earn new familiarity through particular shared interests, repeated chosen
contact, imperfect guesses, and small occasions of accommodation. Keep early
kindness and humor; do not make everyone guarded or introduce mandatory
friction into every conversation. Personal understanding, private disclosures,
assumptions about someone's availability, and shared jokes need local history.

The May 31 outing and Saturday chat involve MC, Matsuda, Tsubaki, and Asahina.
They provide a good day and an invitation to repeat it, with no next date set.
June still needs connections involving Monika and Sam and reasons for newer
pairs to seek each other without MC or Matsuda arranging every encounter.
A defined group need not mean equal closeness across every pair.

The declared dependency route retains its deliberately faster attention and
convenience. Do not flatten that contrast while calibrating ordinary friendship.
See the [revision history](../history/story-revisions.md) and current [Act 1 plans](../project/roadmap.md).

### MC and Matsuda's long arc

Act 1's central trajectory is MC's increasingly overdependent friendship with
Matsuda. Her escalating access to his time and attention contributes to social
isolation, strain, and eventual rupture.

Matsuda is not a simple villain. Her theatricality, insecurity, intrusive
behavior, genuine kindness, loyalty, delight in spectacle, intelligence, and
meta-awareness must remain simultaneously true. Later harm must complicate the
warmth already present instead of declaring that every good moment was false.

MC does not escape through a sudden therapeutic revelation. He can rationalize
the relationship for a long time and recognize an exit only when an event gives
him permission to take one. That delay is part of his characterization.

The older roadmap describes MC as the narrator and Matsuda as the story's
"actual protagonist." Retain that as large-scale context, not a license to make
every other character an accessory to her plot.

## Narrative lens

The main prose is close first-person present tense through MC.

- Bare narration records his observation and immediate experience.
- A fully italicized narration box normally records private thought: the
  admission, correction, joke, or reaction he does not say aloud.
- `nier` interstitials compress travel, elapsed time, classes, chores, and
  montage. They retain MC's diction and are a presentation register, not a
  separate character.

MC is an autistic seventeen-year-old: gifted, observant, dryly funny, and
inclined to play down social cues and emotional beats so they need not become
his problem. He may miss a cue, register it late, or notice it and give himself
an easier explanation. Observation must not automatically make him socially
perceptive or willing to engage. His narration can move onto a concrete task
while the reader still has the uncomfortable observation in mind.

He can use a joke, a practical classification, or a familiar subject to avoid
owning what he feels. Legal or game language belongs where it is natural to
him; it is not a compulsory filter for every experience. These are this
character's habits. Do not substitute a generic autism mannerism, constant
literal misunderstanding, or diagnostic exposition for his particular voice.

His care can outrun what he admits to himself: he carries, fetches, cooks,
notices, remembers, and makes room, then calls it convenient or changes the
subject. An honest admission remains possible when a scene earns it. Do not
turn every emotional exchange into either a perfect response or an identical
'not my problem' punchline.

### Observation without verdict

The standing narration rule is:

> MC may notice. He may not conclude on the reader's behalf.

Keep the concrete evidence and remove the appended answer key. A stopped hand,
careful bite, held smile, adjusted sleeve, closed notebook, second cup, rinsed
plate, or facedown phone can carry its own subtext. When space is reclaimed,
spend it on another concrete observation or narrative thread rather than a new
interpretive verdict.

Referent choice also tracks MC's distance:

- A name means he re-notices, assesses, catalogues, or emotionally retreats.
- A pronoun keeps him inside a continuous moment with the person.
- A role epithet means he is seeing the role, with warmth or irony determined
  by context.
- A body or object anchor narrows attention and can signal intimacy or tension.

Perception is not wisdom. MC needs sincere uncertainty, blind spots, delayed
understanding, and occasionally consequentially wrong readings.

## Textbox and sentence rhythm

Every Ren'Py statement is a textbox, not a paragraph in a manuscript.

- Treat two substantial sentences as an ordinary ceiling, never as a target or
  a template. Do not manufacture a short statement-and-clarification pair just
  to make a box feel complete.
- Judge cadence across consecutive boxes and the whole exchange. A single
  sentence with natural clause movement can hold a slow moment better than two
  clipped sentences followed by a reset.
- **Compound and complex sentences are required parts of the prose's range.**
  The author explicitly identified their avoidance as a must-fix defect on
  September 5. Connect actions, qualifications, causes, and observations when
  MC experiences them together; use coordination and subordination naturally
  in dialogue as well. Do not replace a short-sentence quota with a long one.
- Do not split a continuous utterance at every clause or sentence boundary.
  A click changes time and emphasis, so repetitive fragments can accelerate or
  exhaust a quiet scene even when each box is short.
- Three or more sentences are reserved for short fragments, teaching material,
  performance, or deliberate emotional/comic cadence.
- Never merge dialogue with narration.
- Merge adjacent boxes only when speaker, mode, subject, and emotional timing
  genuinely continue.
- Preserve a separate box when the click supplies breathing room, hesitation,
  reaction time, or an emotional turn; sentence packaging alone is not a beat.
- Let conversation spill through mundane activity, imperfect replies,
  interruptions, and subjects that take time to arrive. Plot information should
  move inside lived exchanges instead of making every line report, explain, or
  conclude its assigned beat.
- Use italics for sparse semantic stress and capitals for real volume.
- Use doubled hyphens for genuine interruption or self-correction, not ordinary
  clause connection. Avoid mechanical semicolons and ornamental punctuation.
- Treat punctuation as the visible trace of speech behavior, not decoration. An
  exclamation point needs actual volume, delight, recoil, or force; an ellipsis
  needs audible delay, uncertainty, or a thought losing momentum; and a doubled
  hyphen needs a cutoff, collision, or correction the player can hear.
- When a clean declarative line suppresses the reaction, rebuild the utterance
  instead of changing only its final mark. Let the character begin the wrong
  sentence, register a sensation midway, interrupt themself, reach for a harsher
  or softer word, and only then arrive at the information.
- Calibrate those behaviors by speaker and scene. Asahina's fast recoil,
  Matsuda's performed volume, Monika's bright momentum, Tsubaki's careful
  restarts, Himeko's controlled stops, and MC's evasive qualifications should
  never be normalized to one shared punctuation density.
- Let emotionally disrupted speech become less polished. An awkward sentence,
  fragment, silence, or plain answer may be more truthful than a finished essay.
- Profanity is welcome where the speaker and pressure earn it, without making
  swearing a shared verbal habit or a quota. The author's September 5 boundary
  excludes the word "fuck" and its inflections, with one explicit exception:
  Himeko's exact exam-guard confrontation line, "Do not insult me in my own
  fucking home by trying to dress this up as pressure." Preserve that line.
  Everywhere else, use an in-character substitute such as "freak," "frick," or Asahina's
  "firetruck," or rebuild the line so its force comes from the delivery.

### Ellipsis house style

Use three adjacent periods with no space before the ellipsis and one ordinary
space before speech continues: `word... word`. An initial hesitation followed
by speech is `... Word`. Keep the existing capitalization when spacing alone
is being corrected; a pause does not automatically start a new sentence.

Line-final `word...` and standalone `...` take no added trailing space. Keep
question/exclamation punctuation attached (`...?`, `...!`) and put the ordinary
space after the punctuation if speech resumes. Formatting tags are invisible:
`word...{i}word{/i}` must render with the same gap as `word... word`. Preserve
Ren'Py interpolation and timing tags when fixing displayed spacing.

This is a consistent typography choice, not a claim that every publisher uses
the same ellipsis style. The [University of Oxford's punctuation guide](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/brand/style-guide/punctuation)
likewise uses a following space when an ellipsis marks a pause for effect.
The scope is pauses in story prose and dialogue; do not apply a prose formatter
to filesystem paths, code tokens, or quoted omissions that need separate review.

### Line-by-line cadence review

Meticulous prose includes connective and procedural boxes, not only emotional
highlights. Review every displayed statement with the boxes immediately before
and after it:

1. Identify the breath, turn of attention, interruption, or changed pressure
   that earns its click. If there is none, merge or reshape the sequence.
2. For dialogue, ask what social concern the speaker answers and whether the
   line could belong to anyone else. Information transfer alone is not a voice.
3. Treat conspicuously short speech as an emphasis decision. In a face-to-face
   exchange, a sustained run of tiny A/B replies requires a specific comic,
   emotional, or professional reason.
4. Make continuous action continuous in syntax, using ordinary conjunctions,
   qualification, false starts, and corrections where the voice calls for it.
5. When dialogue, expression, gesture, and narration all explain the same
   response, keep the strongest combination and remove the answer key.
6. Read the whole scene at textbox speed. Developed, medium, and short breaths
   should vary with the scene rather than settle into one default pulse.

### Monika

The author clarified that "TT" was a residual inside description, not a
reference to external canon. Monika is tired, fed up with people's behavior,
and wants rest from responsibility. In ENTER M she uses her chipper mood and
smile as masks; passive-aggressiveness occasionally slips through.

Her outward brightness, organization, teasing, precision, and energy therefore
need room for friction underneath. A cheerful thank-you can land pointedly when
someone ignores the instructions she has already given; a polite reply can
reveal irritation when another obligation is assigned without asking. Give the
slip a concrete cause and let the wording, timing, or expression carry it.
Frequency follows the scene, not a quota.

MC can notice the reply or what her hand does without concluding that she is
masking. He may answer the practical part, take a remark literally, or decide
it does not need his attention. Preserve the difference between the author's
knowledge of her mask and the narrator's willingness to understand it.

Her warmth, affection, humor, and enjoyment can still be sincere. Time with MC
can give her actual relief from being responsible for everything. Their private
vocabulary includes poems, revisions, brackets, sleeves, windows, tables,
schedules, and second cups. Keep the romance restrained, let her want something
imperfectly, and preserve the cost of competence without converting every
friendly exchange into concealed resentment.

MC and Monika have already been friends for a year. Their ease must include
shared enjoyment and chosen time beyond homework, club administration, and
reassurance. Let MC initiate activities, hold tastes she does not share, and
stay because he is enjoying himself. Show their friendship happening instead
of adding a line that announces they have hobbies. Her teasing need not become
a lesson, and his answer need not become a clever classification.

The author clarified on September 7 that Monika clearly likes MC romantically.
MC is not oblivious to her advances: he notices them but does not give them
attention because he is not interested in pursuing romance with her. Genuine
tenderness and enjoyment of their friendship can coexist with that indifference
to romantic escalation. A scene may invite the player's romantic anticipation
without rewarding it with dating, a confession, or hidden mutual yearning.
Do not explain his non-engagement as confusion, fear, or an inability to read her.

### Asahina

Snobbish, fifteen, and smarter than she lets on. She has tastes and standards,
can judge other people too quickly, and dislikes exposing how much she cares
about getting something right. Let her competence show in what she notices or
does before she dismisses it. She can be fast, abrasive, slangy, profane, funny,
artistic, athletic, and fiercely sensitive to being treated as childish. Her bluntness can be a form of empathy.
The sibling relationship expresses safety through rhythm: they can insult,
interrupt, recruit, feed, annoy, and quietly rescue one another without turning
every caring act into a lesson.

Her food/body strain remains behavioral and unresolved. Portions, reported
meals, arrangement, avoidance, athletic identity, praise, maturity, and shame
may accumulate, but narration must not diagnose her or make victimhood her only
identity.

### Himeko

Perceptive, practical, warm, and slightly hands-off about ordinary teenage
life. She can notice a dodge and let it pass, leave room for a mistake, and
expect her children to manage things she knows they can manage. Meals, notes,
errands, and affection need not become repeated supervision or questioning.

When things get bad, she intervenes decisively. Her precision, restraint, and
knowledge of the person can make a sentence devastating without a physical
threat or a speech about her profession. The exam confrontation earns that
severity; routine home scenes should not operate at the same pressure. Retain
her blind spots and capacity to misjudge, alongside her perceptiveness.

### Matsuda

Theatrical, well-meaning, maximalist, magnetic, imaginative, and physically
staged. Her constant role-playing can make an honest invitation or useful act
feel overbearing; the performance can take more room than she realizes.
Archaic and chuuni registers belong to particular acts, rather than every line. Her performance is both sincere pleasure and a way to control
the first interpretation of an event. Plain breaks in the register matter.
Preserve her ability to be funny, exhausting, kind, and genuinely fun. Do not
flatten her into "loud girl secretly sad" or telegraph her as a villain.

down into interchangeable banter. Sustain each act through vocabulary, sentence

immediately reverting to generic fantasy talk.

### Tsubaki

Reserved, with her own backbone and sense of humor. She often translates
Matsuda's performance into the practical thing being said or offered, but can
also play along, refuse, tease, or have an agenda of her own. She has dry corrections,
restrained annoyance, competence, literary and design interests, specific wants,
and spine around Matsuda's health or excess. Do not translate every gesture into
an insecurity diagnosis. Her agency must eventually include the right to be
inconvenient, mistaken, angry, or wanted for more than usefulness.

### Sam

A pretty boy who is socially intelligent, relaxed, charming, and capable of
being a bro. He reads the room and can offer company, practical help, or a joke
without making it an emotional consultation. Give him his own ordinary interests
and let some of his attention simply be friendship. His care may
develop a deniable resemblance to tracking or management, but main-story
evidence has earned suspicion rather than conviction. Preserve genuine
investment and affection so later danger concerns his model of care, not a
retroactive claim that every kindness was fraudulent.

### Kotonoha

Matter-of-fact, minimally expressive, precise, and professionally competent.
She need not smile broadly or react emotionally to prove that she is present. Her many
jobs are a completely earnest Nurse Joy-style in-universe running gag. Nobody
questions the premise. Her humor comes from exactness and timing, not from
knowing she is the joke. Small earned warmth is valid; broad performance and
premature uncanny emptiness are not.

## Atmosphere and emotional method

Build atmosphere from intimate ordinary evidence: afternoon dust, refrigerator
hum, page noise, frying oil, bus windows, classroom silence, track heat,
receipts, cups, wrappers, bags, chairs, food, and light under a door.

Emotion should remain embedded in conduct and environment:

- romance through chosen attention, shared work, private motifs, and reciprocal
  teasing;
- family love through food, notes, chargers, water, rides, and household labor;
- strain through changing routine, omission, posture, and what remains after a
  person leaves;
- performance through Matsuda's volume, shoulders, staging, and code-switching;
- dependence through who acts, who asks, and whose notebook or plan receives
  the action.

Give a meaningful line room to exist. The next beat may be silence, a concrete
reaction, an unfinished action, or a deliberately ordinary continuation. Do not
automatically rescue vulnerability with a bell, phone, door, arrival, or joke.

## Intrigue and pacing

Quiet does not mean static. Each substantial scene should normally change at
least one of the following:

- what the player understands about a character;
- what one character believes about another;
- a relationship's comfort, boundary, obligation, or private language;
- a live practical pressure;
- the meaning of an earlier object, line, or routine;
- the question the player carries into a later scene.

Pacing is natural when these particular people could reach the beat in real
time. Intimacy must be earned. Discoveries, invitations, disclosures, entrances,
and interruptions need local character causes. Conflict should allow reaction,
resistance, misunderstanding, repair, and residue instead of yielding to the
first perfect diagnosis.

Breathing room is active when it lets the player register a changed behavior,
compare it to an earlier routine, or form an interpretation. It becomes drag
when it repeats the scene's conclusion, supplies another thematic metaphor, or
recaps travel and cleanup without changing a relationship or question.

Intrigue should be carried by character-specific residue rather than arbitrary
cliffhangers. Preserve unanswered but legible questions: why a routine changed,
why a character chose that phrasing, what somebody noticed and did not name,
whether a kindness is also management, or why an ordinary object now feels
different.

## Choices

Author direction, September 21: a choice may change what happens, including
an event, a location, who is present, or what someone decides. The earlier
rule that menus could only select tone, and its list of exceptions, is
retired.

Chapter 0.5's existing menus mostly select tone, attention, or honesty and
converge. Later prose may remember which version of MC the player inhabited,
and these particular choices are never punished as guesses.

The dependency route keeps its own ledger and contract. Its substitution
branch must feel faster, warmer, and more attentive so the later meaning
concerns a repeated chosen pattern, not obvious cruelty. Preserve the
all-or-nothing chain and every abort condition.

## Revision hazards

The most common excess is an overexplaining ladder:

1. A gesture or object shows the feeling.
2. Narration names it.
3. Dialogue diagnoses it.
4. A joke or metaphor reframes it.
5. The scene lands.
6. A transition or thought ledger records it again.

Most scenes need one or two of those rungs. Keep the most concrete and
character-specific version.

Also guard against:

- several elegant endings after the scene has already ended;
- a third joke after the strongest reply;
- every character speaking with polished emotional fluency;
- classroom material that annotates the plot too perfectly;
- `X enough to Y` calibration and appended verdicts;
- repeated negated definitions, triads, measured micro-pauses, stale ellipses,
  and text-about-text language such as "the line lands";
- routine transition inventories that do not alter understanding;
- replacing a repeated phrase with a synonym while leaving the duplicate
  dramatic job intact.

The governing edit principle is: remove repetition, not life. Preserve the
daily architecture, quiet scenes, family meals, errands, calls, school texture,
restrained Monika romance, Matsuda's excess, character privacy, and uncertainty.

## Feedback standard from the author, September 5

Be ruthless and explicit about grammar, spelling, continuity, dialogue, story
quality, felt pacing, worldbuilding, enjoyment, and dissatisfaction. Identify
the actual defect and its effect, with a concrete example when useful. Separate
what was changed from proposals and what source checks establish from what
requires playback or reader response. Do not invent flaws to fill a category,
or describe every change as an improvement merely because an agent made it.
