When revising ENTER M, you do NOT have to make surgical, minimal-footprint edits. The user is completely fine with an interaction's wording changing entirely — rewrite dialogue and narration wholesale, restructure an exchange, replace whole runs of lines — as long as the intent and purpose of the beat are preserved.

**Why:** The user observed that I default to extremely precise, conservative insertions and tiny "improvements," and explicitly asked for more freedom. They care about intent / purpose / effect, not about preserving the original phrasing. (Surfaced right after the exam-arc + confrontation pass, where several fixes were small targeted swaps that still "read basically the same.")

**How to apply:** When a scene needs work, prioritize the effect the beat is reaching for over keeping its exact words — rebuild it from the ground up if that serves it better. What must still be preserved regardless: story continuity and planted seeds; character voice and the established registers ([[enter-m-character-voices]]); the house-style rules — restraint, show-don't-tell, fragment economy, music transitions ([[enter-m-voice-fingerprint]]); and the Ren'Py machinery (character tags, labels, `$`/menu/jump/call, persistent flags, phone helpers — only prose inside quotes is fair game). The freedom is over WORDING, not over mechanics or intent.

**Punctuation & animation (DIALOGUE) — added 2026-06-17:** The dialogue currently leans too hard on flat periods, which makes characters read like static PNGs on a screen instead of people with voices. Punctuate dialogue to convey HOW a line is actually said: exclamation points for real energy, incredulous or rising question marks, em dashes for interruptions and mid-thought self-corrections, ellipses for hesitation and trailing off; let characters cut each other off, change direction mid-sentence, lose the thread, get loud. The reader should hear vocal energy and a body behind the words.
- This is about liveliness in DIALOGUE and does NOT conflict with the narration fragment-economy rule (that one targets staccato *narration* like "Not x. Not y. Z."). Lively dialogue, cleaner-flowing narration.
- Do not over-apply or turn it into exclamation spam. Exceptions: Kotonoha's flat periods are DELIBERATE (uncanny, professionally vacant — keep her clipped); Mom's clamped/controlled register and genuinely somber or tender beats earn period-heavy restraint (see the restraint rule). Match the punctuation to the emotional truth of the moment — which, for the lively characters (Asahina, Matsuda, playful Monika, MC mid-banter), is almost always more varied than a row of periods.
