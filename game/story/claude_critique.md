# PART 1 — Blind Reader Critique

*Scope: the main story path only, disclaimer through May 28. No add-ons, no planning notes, no author context. I am reacting to what the screen actually told me.*

## Rating

**8/10 in its current state.**

I am bumping this half a point above where I expected to land when the week started, and the reason is the back third. The first nine or ten days are a warm, talky, sometimes over-clever slice-of-life that I enjoyed but could feel coasting. Then May 23 (the college conversation), May 24-25 (the Monika library/cafe stretch), and the May 26-28 exam arc arrive, and the writing stops *performing* competence and starts *demonstrating* it. The exam days in particular are the most disciplined, tonally controlled material in the build: long, quiet, restrained, and genuinely tense without a single character cracking a defensive joke to bail out the moment. That stretch is what an 8.5 looks like. The earlier days drag it back toward 7.5. I averaged honestly and landed on 8.

What keeps it from going higher is the same thing the story keeps proving it can fix: a reflexive joke habit that defuses tender beats, a cast that expands faster than the central engine declares itself, and a literature club that keeps telling me writing matters while skipping the writing — until, notably, it finally stops skipping it on May 24.

## Overall Reader Experience

For two weeks this reads as a grounded high-school life-sim with a soft romance, a family/coming-of-age thread, a returning track subplot, and a faint uncanny frame around the edges (the Nier-style disclaimer and interstitials, the May 22 waiting-room dream, Kotonoha's unnerving precision). The calendar structure makes it easy to follow, and by the end I had a clear sense of the active pressures:

- Monika quietly wanting unstructured time with MC before the school year swallows it (May 24 rooftop, May 25 library/cafe).
- MC facing graduation, a specific college, and the guilt of possibly leaving home (May 23, threaded through May 25).
- Asahina performing confidence over real insecurity — the poster, the food beats, "do not tell Sam I needed help" (May 25).
- Matsuda pushing too fast, then proving she can hear "stop" and "slow down" (May 19, May 23).
- Tsubaki wanting permission to occupy space without apologizing for it.
- Sam being almost suspiciously smooth, and Mom warning that attention is not the same as safety.
- Midterms and track as concrete, time-eating pressure that finally pays off across May 26-28.

That is a strong, coherent set of threads, and crucially, by the end of the window they start *colliding* instead of just coexisting. The May 24 gym scene puts Asahina, Sam, Tsubaki, and Monika in MC's orbit in one morning; May 25 has Monika gently interrogating MC about Tsubaki and Sam over lunch. The story is learning to make one scene change how I read the next.

## What Works Best

### The exam arc (May 26-28) is the build's high point

This is the most confident writing in the game. Three days, six subjects, and the script resists every temptation it usually gives in to. There are no rescue-jokes after the hard beats. The home scenes are quiet and specific — Asahina silently fixing a glass on the wrong side of the plate, Mom turning a water bottle so the cap faces MC and saying nothing, the "morale ice cream" she pretends is "randomized distribution." The exam questions themselves double as thematic echoes (the unreliable-narrator and omitted-agency questions land on a protagonist the story has been quietly framing as both), and MC's internal asides during the papers ("restraint is how people keep a room from noticing blood on the floor") are sharper than his usual quips because they are *not* trying to be funny.

The May 28 ending — no studying in the club room, a fist bump with Asahina, uneven ice cream, "later is allowed to stay later" — is the best closing beat in the build. It feels earned because the week actually cost something.

### The college conversation (May 23)

This is the scene the earlier draft of the story keeps reaching toward. Mom does not tell MC the theme; she helps him *handle* it — deadlines, requirements, commute, housing, the "optional" portfolio that is not optional, the merit-scholarship question mark. And it gets concrete in exactly the way the rest of the future-pressure thread had been avoiding: a named field (computer science, cybersecurity track), a real commute (two hours by train plus bus), specific courses MC visibly leans toward despite himself. MC's line — "I said something too loudly and the world started taking notes" — is one of the best in the script. The scene works because it lets the discomfort sit. Mom's "Distance and love are not opposites" is plain and lands.

### Monika's late vulnerability (May 24-25)

Monika is at her strongest when she has to risk asking for something. The May 24 rooftop invitation — studying as the stated reason, "I missed talking to you without the school day pulling at our sleeves" as the real one, and the devastating "Thanks. / For saying yes? / For not making me make it smaller" — is the most alive she feels in the whole build. May 25 sustains it: the library study session is paced like real studying, but the small honest beats ("Friend order," "I like knowing what shape the hard thing is," "For you" instead of "against you") accumulate into something that reads as a date neither of them will name. The matching window-table poems on May 24 are a genuinely good idea executed well.

### The poems finally appear (May 24)

For nine days the club kept summarizing the writing. On May 24 the script finally *shows* it — MC's six defensive lines about the bus seat ("The seat remembers nobody, which is why it is kind") and Monika's "Table Near The Window" reply, with the shared "pressure changes shape" motif. This single scene does more for the MC/Monika relationship than all the earlier banter combined, and it retroactively justifies the club existing. It is proof of what the story should have been doing all along.

### Domestic texture and Mom

The home scenes remain the foundation. Mom is the most consistently effective character: adult without being a flat wise-parent, with a recognizable legal/counselor flavor ("Counselor mom, actually. Doctor mom bills more"). The household runs on specific, lived-in details — sticky notes, saved plates, charger disputes, three places set at the table by default with one covered for Mom. The May 25 study scene between MC and Asahina ("I hate when math gets reasonable after I hate it," the recycled two-year-old science packet) is warm and genuinely funny without being mean.

### Track

The track scenes are excellent when they stay physical: overstriding, creeping shoulders, "land under the hip," the difference between effort and restraint. Coach has bite without overperforming, and he gives MC a form of pressure that is not social. "We can't have you becoming the team cripple" lands harder than the form notes because it is the one time Coach stops being funny.

### Kotonoha

Kotonoha is handled with discipline. Her strangeness lives in behavior — dodging the car splash before it lands, tightening a loose glue cap, folding a receipt exactly down the middle, "Most people watch where cars were." By May 25 she has quietly appeared as glue clerk, theater concessions, *and* cafe barista, and the script plays it straight: "You attend my school," "Luck does not replace preparation. But customers prefer the phrase." She is uncanny without being asked to be cryptic, which is the right call.

## What Needs The Most Work

### The first nine days lean on the joke reflex too hard

The single most repeated problem in the early build: the script lands a tender or tense beat and then immediately reaches for a joke, nickname, or insult, training me not to sit with anything. Monika's sleeve-grab in the movie becomes an instant retort chain. The May 21 phone call buries its actual emotional point (Monika is trying to make MC sound less tired) under a very long cup-conspiracy bit and a spaghetti-is-a-salad debate. The good news is the back third proves the team *knows* how to stop doing this — the exam arc and the library scenes are full of beats the script lets breathe. The fix is to retroactively apply that restraint to the early days.

**Concrete fix:** after an emotionally revealing line in the May 15-22 material, let two or three lines pass without a punchline. The story already does this beautifully later; it just needs to trust itself earlier.

### The cast expands faster than the central promise declares itself

By May 17 I had met Monika, Mom, Asahina, Kotonoha, Sam, Matsuda, and Tsubaki, plus teachers and Coach, and Hikari/Homura arrive mid-argument on May 22 as if I already knew them. For most of the first two weeks I understood the *people* better than I understood what kind of story I was being asked to emotionally prepare for. Is it primarily romance? A social-drama web? A coming-of-age about leaving home? The uncanny frame? It can be several, but the early experience needs a sharper sense of which one is load-bearing. (The exam arc finally gives me a candidate spine — honesty, pressure, and consequence — but that is day 12.)

### Hikari and Homura are imported, not seeded

Their May 22 club argument is a strong meta-scene about critique and charity, but they appear attached to a theme rather than as people I know. Until then the club has functionally been MC-plus-Monika. One identifying line in an earlier club scene would make the conflict feel like a known tension surfacing instead of two strangers arriving with a lesson.

### MC's narration flattens the world into one register (early)

MC is a strong protagonist — observant, sarcastic, self-protective. But in the early days nearly everything (teachers, family, dreams, track, romance) gets processed through the same polished joke, which makes him feel less like a teenager reacting in real time and more like a narrator who revised every thought afterward. Again, the later days fix this: the college scene and the exam papers let him be inarticulate, plain, or wrong. That variation should reach back into the opening.

### Some sibling jokes cross from bite into casual cruelty

The Asahina dynamic is mostly warm, but a few lines briefly make MC read meaner than the surrounding affection supports: "fatass" and "bagged obesity" on May 15, "I'd rather kill myself" over a poster on May 16, the boiling-water threat on May 21, "Maybe you'd be more popular if you talked eighty percent less" on May 19. Individually small; collectively they nick the trust the family scenes otherwise build. If MC is meant to occasionally wound, let him notice. If not, space them out.

### The dream is caught between eerie and gag reel (May 22)

The waiting room, blank ticket, classroom purgatory, cup, open door, and conveyor belt of responsibilities are strong, genuinely unsettling images. The joke class names ("Quantum Rizzics," "Advanced Organic Scientology") and the density of quips undercut the subconscious pressure. The dream should be the story's hidden machinery briefly surfacing; right now it keeps apologizing for being strange.

## Character-Specific Notes

### MC

Engaging and easy to follow; his best trait is that he reads other people closely (Monika's habits, Asahina's deflection, Sam's smoothness, Tsubaki's caution). His weakness in the early days is that he is too emotionally armored by narration — he notices the right thing, jokes, and moves on. By May 23-28 the armor cracks productively: he can't joke his way out of the college talk, and during exams he narrates his own avoidance ("restraint is how people keep a room from noticing blood on the floor") in a way that suggests he knows more about himself than he admits. That version of MC is the strong one. Watch his harshness with Asahina.

### Monika

Charming but, for the first half, charming in a single mode: teasing, cute labels, responsibility jokes. She becomes far stronger when she wants something and risks asking — the rooftop invitation, the library day, "For not making me make it smaller." Her relationship with MC is believable; what the late material finally adds is *private specificity* (the matching poems, the shared "pressure changes shape" image), which is exactly what it needed. Keep building from May 24-25, not from the May 20 text banter.

### Asahina

One of the most vivid characters, and the May 25 study scene is her best showing. Her pattern — insult, deny, embarrassment, soft reveal, insult — is a real sibling rhythm but can get predictable; the scenes that complicate it (the charger apology, "do not tell Sam I needed help," "He would be nice about it... probably too nice, which is worse") are the strongest. Her food/eating beats stand out enough across the build (the single pork wedge and spoon of rice on May 15, "I'm not that hungry today," fast-food jokes, athlete-body comments) that I assumed an intentional thread. If it is, handle it carefully and don't let MC's narration wave it off as "nothing worth making a fuss over" unless that dismissal is clearly his blind spot.

### Sam

Pleasant, readable, and — deliberately or not — passing every social test a little too cleanly. He fixes Asahina's sleeve cuff, supplies "Boyfriend" gently, reads Tsubaki accurately in thirty seconds ("Like she thinks being noticed and being in the way are the same thing"), cleans up without being asked. As a blind reader I clocked him as *almost certainly* hiding something precisely because nothing is ever slightly off about him — and the story seems to want that (Mom's attention-isn't-safety warning, MC's own "too calm to dismiss, which is inconvenient"). The risk is that "perfectly smooth" without a single visible wrong note can read as authorial thumb-on-scale rather than character. One tiny ambiguous beat — a compliment landing a half-second too targeted, a glance held a beat too long — would let me distrust him *from the page* instead of from genre instinct.

### Tsubaki

Very promising; her insecurity is behavioral, which is the right way to do it (apologizing for being a witness, asking permission to sit in a public space, the delayed smiles, pressing a palm over her notebook). Her line that MC and Asahina "don't sound like you're checking every sentence before you're allowed to say it" is one of the best emotional cuts in the build. Avoid over-diagnosing her through dialogue ("anxiety levels and depth of performance are directly proportional" leans that way) — she's stronger when she reveals herself by accident.

### Matsuda

Memorable immediately, but her debut (May 17) is so dense with performance that it nearly tires me out before I can care what's underneath. The scenes that save her are the ones where the mask slips: the May 19 hallway "okay, stop" boundary, and especially the May 23 text exchange where she drops the bard entirely — "I overstepped," "I was hoping if I acted like it had been longer, it would feel less embarrassing." That plainness is more compelling than any ornate entrance, and the script seems to know it. Use the theatrical voice as seasoning, not the whole meal.

### Kotonoha

Strong and disciplined (see above). The only caution: she's accumulating cryptic one-liners ("You will," "Most people watch where cars were"). Her precision is more interesting than her mystery; ration the cryptic lines so they stay special.

## Tone And Atmosphere

The atmosphere is strongest when it is quiet, warm, and faintly pressurized — ordinary life with too many tabs open. There's also an uncanny/systemic layer (the Nier disclaimer and interstitials, the dream, Kotonoha, the calendar, the recurring lessons about agency and performance). Early on, that layer felt like a cool frame laid *over* a grounded slice-of-life rather than growing from inside it. The exam arc is where the two finally fuse: the academic-honesty pressure is both completely ordinary *and* the place where the story's interest in agency, omission, and consequence becomes literal. The comedy is a real strength and a real liability — it gives the script personality but, when overused, prevents depth. The late material's best discovery is letting a joke be the thing a character *uses to avoid* the hard thing, with the hard thing still visible underneath.

## Pacing And Structure

The day-by-day rhythm is easy to follow but, in the early stretch, predictable: morning routine, class quiz scene, club/social encounter, home scene, night reflection/texts, calendar advance. The class-quiz scenes especially settle into a formula (teacher explains, MC quips, MC gets called on for looking attentive, player picks an answer, correct/correction, summary). The exam arc breaks the formula hard and to great effect — three consecutive days built around the same structural spine but escalating in stakes, with the quiz mechanic finally feeling like it *matters* rather than being a comprehension check. May 23 onward is the strongest run because the threads interlock instead of sitting beside each other.

One structural note as a player: the May 26-28 exam questions are presented as interactive menus, and (refreshingly) picking a "wrong" answer doesn't punish you — MC simply walks his reasoning back to the right answer in narration. That's a smart, low-anxiety way to keep the player engaged through six exams without turning the climax into a pop quiz you can fail. It does mean the choices are functionally cosmetic, which is fine here because the *drama* isn't in whether you get matrices right.

## What Probably Falls Flat

- **The disclaimer** remains stiff and repetitive as a first impression. If the Nier voice is part of the identity, make even the warning feel intentional and atmospheric; if it's pure compliance text, shorten it.
- **The May 22 dream**, as above — great images, too many gags.
- **The long banter chains** in the first half (the May 21 phone call is the prime offender) outstay their natural endpoint because every character keeps finding one more punchline.
- **Dated internet phrasing** — "chosen violence," "skill issue," spelled-out "en-gee-el"/"ngl," "brotato chip" — is funny now but timestamps the voice. The late material uses much less of it and is better for it.
- **Some proofreading nits** are visible on the page: "porkloins," "dumbell," a stray "!." on May 20, "Hey, don't cut cheap out sometimes" on May 18 reading like a draft error, and a few tense slips. Minor, but they stand out against otherwise clean prose.

## UX / Visual Novel Notes

- The phone UI is a genuine asset for showing asynchronous pressure and social overlap (Matsuda's texts crowding in beside Monika's is the earliest visual version of MC's attention getting contested). But long phone conversations should earn their length through escalation or a decision; the May 21 call is too long for what it accomplishes.
- The class-answer menus read as quizzes more than choices early on. The exam-arc versions work better because the *context* (stakes, consequence, repetition) makes the act of answering feel weighted even when the outcome is fixed.
- The sticky-note beats are currently rendered as italic narration that can blur into MC's internal voice. A dev comment on May 23 already flags wanting a sticky-note image; I'd endorse that — it would make the notes visually distinct and reinforce Mom's presence-by-proxy.
- The build no longer ends on a "tech demo" cliff mid-relationship; May 28 is a real, satisfying chapter close. That's a meaningful improvement in how the current build lands.

## Priority Revision Suggestions

1. **Push the late-material restraint backward into May 15-22.** The exam arc proves the team can let beats breathe; apply that discipline to the early days.
2. **Declare the central engine sooner.** Give the first week a clearer sense of which theme is load-bearing (the honesty/consequence thread the exams crystallize is a strong candidate).
3. **Seed Hikari and Homura before their argument.** One earlier line is enough.
4. **Trim the long banter chains,** especially the May 21 call. Pick the best joke in a sequence and cut the second-best.
5. **Rework the dream toward unease over comedy.** The images are good; trust them.
6. **Give Sam one visible wrong note** so distrust comes from the page, not from genre instinct.
7. **Watch the harsher sibling/violence jokes;** the family tone is warm enough that they briefly make MC read cruel.
8. **Reduce the dated slang** to protect the voice's shelf life.
9. **Keep showing the writing in the club** (May 24 is the model); don't relapse into summarizing it.
10. **Build more "morale ice cream" beats** — the quiet, specific, joke-free family moments are the build's emotional engine, and the back third proves it.

## Final Takeaway (Part 1)

I cared about MC's home life, I believed Monika liked him, I wanted to know what Tsubaki was missing, I distrusted Sam on instinct, and the college thread gave the slice-of-life structure a real horizon. What surprised me is how much stronger the story got in its second half. The first nine days are a charming, slightly over-caffeinated 7.5; the back stretch — the college talk, the Monika library day, and above all the three-day exam arc — is the work of a writer who has figured out that quiet and specific beat clever and constant. If the whole build matched the confidence of May 26-28, this would be a clear 8.5 or higher. As it stands, it's a genuine 8, carried over the line by an ending that finally lets its best moments land without a joke to cushion them.

# PART 2 — Addendum: Second-Pass Critique With Author Context

*This section was written after reading the roadmap, roster profiles, archive/character stories, dev scenes, and intended-voice notes. It does **not** replace Part 1. The blind pass still matters because it reflects what the public-facing script communicates on its own. This pass revisits where author intent changes — or confirms — my blind complaints, extended to cover May 25-28.*

## Revised Rating Context

Judged purely as a first-time-reader experience, the current main story holds at my blind **8/10**.

Knowing the blueprint, I'd put the project's long-form potential at **9/10** — higher than I expected going in, because the plan is genuinely more ambitious and more coherent than the public slice can prove on its own. The reason for the optimism is specific: almost every blind-read complaint I had turns out to be load-bearing for a later payoff, *and* the team has already demonstrated (in the exam arc, the college scene, the dev scenes) that it can execute the disciplined version. The gap between 8 and 9 is entirely an execution risk, not an idea problem. If the slow burn stays as controlled as May 26-28, this lands near 9. If the comedy reflex creeps back and the darkness ends up justified by author notes rather than visible accumulation, the same plan could stall around 7.

The single most important thing the author context changed for me: **this is not primarily a romance or a slice-of-life.** Matsuda is the actual protagonist and is meta-aware; Monika is being deliberately faded toward an implied death; Tsubaki is scheduled to deteriorate and effectively vanish; Mom gets a rare cancer; MC is an explicitly unreliable narrator whose performance decline is meant to be *narrated away* by MC himself so the reader half-misses it. The current build is the first half of a prologue to a five-chapter Act 1 that runs May 15, 2025 to March 7, 2026, followed by a dual-perspective Act 2 and a detective-thriller intermission. That reframes the "cast expands too fast / promise unclear" complaint enormously — but, crucially, does not dissolve it.

## The Prologue Framing Recontextualizes "Too Slow / Too Crowded"

The roadmap says Act 1 chapters are each self-contained episodes with an underlying continuity that "doesn't really become apparent until chapter 3," and that the true nature of the story unravels at the end of chapter 3. So my blind worry — "I understand the cast better than the engine" — is partly *intended*: the engine is supposed to stay hidden. That's a legitimate design choice, and the exam arc shows it can pay off.

But the blind complaint still has teeth, because a fair slow burn hides the *interpretation*, not the *evidence*. The danger is that "the reader shouldn't notice yet" slides into "the reader isn't given enough to notice in hindsight." The fix isn't to declare the theme early; it's to make sure every quiet day moves at least one hidden meter so that, on a reread, the player can say "it was happening the whole time" instead of "the story changed." The good news: the build already does this in places (Matsuda's texts crowding Monika's on May 17; the food beats around Asahina; MC narrating his own avoidance during exams). The note is to make it *systematic* across the daily-life days the roadmap admits will exist.

## MC's Unreliability Is Confirmed — Make It Behavioral, Not Announced

The roster and roadmap confirm MC is "unstable and unreliable," emotionally numb, and prone to disappearing into usefulness. My blind read flagged that his narration is too composed and too clever; intent says that composure *is* the flaw. So the answer is not to make him dumber or less funny — it's to let his intelligence fail in emotionally specific ways. The exam arc already nails this: "restraint is how people keep a room from noticing blood on the floor" is MC telling on himself. The `dev_mom_cancer_reveal` scene nails it even harder — Mom explicitly names his impulse ("your job is not to become a third parent," "you do not get to disappear into responsibility so you can avoid feeling any of it"). That scene is the thesis of the character. The earlier days should plant the same behavior in miniature: let "it was fine" be contradicted by what the scene shows, so the reader learns to distrust his summary before the plot requires it.

This also retroactively justifies a Part 1 instinct I want to flag: the roadmap's chapter-3 note explicitly wants MC's decline in health/performance to be "brushed off narratively (like MC doesn't want the reader to pay attention to that fact)." That is a sophisticated, achievable effect — but it only works if the reader has been taught, in the prologue, that MC's narration omits things. The bus-seat poem on May 24 ("The seat remembers nobody, which is why it is kind / No one asks whether I am doing well in the reflection") is the best existing seed for this. Plant more like it.

## Matsuda: The Blind Read Was Working Exactly As Designed

This is the biggest recontextualization. Blind, I read Matsuda as an exhausting performer with a real person underneath, and I distrusted nothing beyond "she pushes too fast." The roadmap reveals she is the *actual protagonist*, meta-aware like Monika, more intellectually gifted than anyone (including Monika), and deliberately playing a "clown" role she knows is a role — with intentional *gaps in her acting*. The May 23 rooftop scene already plants this beautifully and I underrated it blind: "her eyes flick from Tsubaki to Asahina to me, counting the group before the group has agreed to be counted," and the moment her performance "tightens around her instead of flowing out of her." That's an acting gap. So is the lukewarm "...Thank ya" when Tsubaki hands her the rice cracker — plainness breaking through the bard.

The craft guidance from Part 1 stands and is now sharper: keep her exhausting, but make sure the *reading* of her isn't, and seed the acting gaps as deliberate tells (a lukewarm reaction where intensity is expected; a too-precise read under a ridiculous speech; the swimsuit-calm immodesty the rambling notes mention). The roadmap's own note — that MC needs a realistic *excuse* to escape the toxic friendship rather than a clean realization, because that's how people actually leave toxic relationships — is psychologically astute and should govern how the prologue plants MC's growing accommodation of her.

## Sam: "Purgatorium" Confirms the Blind Distrust — and Is the Biggest Unlock-Timing Risk

My blind read clocked Sam as "almost certainly hiding something, precisely because nothing is ever off." Intent confirms it: he's "conniving," hides it from MC and Asahina, and is the catalyst for the Asahina and Tsubaki tragedies. The Sam character story "Purgatorium" is a genuinely excellent, disturbing x-ray of his psychology — a bootleg dating-sim where he watches only the Desire and Dependency stats, skips the girl's interiority, screenshots her on Day 1, learns her favorite food but never shares his, and the Day 7 entity indicts him for treating real women (the five-message girl) exactly the way he treats the game: silence as leverage, "as long as she wasn't sure, she wasn't gone." It is thematically airtight and ties directly to the `sam_encounter_99` dev scene (asking MC to spy on Asahina) and the `dev_tsubaki_completely_ruined` deterioration.

This produces a clear rule for main-story Sam, and validates my Part 1 fix: **he should treat people like interfaces while sounding like a boyfriend.** His May 24 read of Tsubaki ("Like she thinks being noticed and being in the way are the same thing") is exactly right — plausible concern that is actually targeting acquisition. My one Part 1 suggestion (give him a single visible wrong note) is the right call, but with a caveat the author context adds: **"Purgatorium" is the most spoiler-sensitive item in the archive.** If it unlocks too early it converts Sam from ambiguous social threat into known predator and flattens the main-story tension. It should be gated late — ideally only once the main story has already made the player distrust him. The current archive appears to unlock character stories on *meeting* the character (`mark_character_met`), and the disclaimer label force-unlocks everything for testing; for release, Sam's story specifically should be event-gated, not presence-gated.

## Tsubaki: The Biggest Retroactive Weight, and Her Story Is Still a Stub

Blind, Tsubaki was "promising, behaviorally insecure." Intent makes her the emotional center of Act 1's tragedy: she undergoes visible deterioration (the `tsubaki post` sprite), has an unrequited crush on Sam she hides to protect Asahina's relationship, and by the final chapter is meant to vanish from the narrative so completely that "not even the story acknowledges her anymore." The `dev_tsubaki_completely_ruined` scene is devastating and lands precisely because the prologue established a clean, specific baseline — neat notes, hygiene, apologizing for taking space, the yellow notebook she guards with one hand. That contrast is the whole engine of her arc.

So the prologue work on her is doing exactly what it must, and my Part 1 note ("don't over-diagnose her through dialogue") matters more now, not less: the deterioration will read as loss only if the baseline is shown through behavior, not labeled. One concrete gap to flag for the author: her character story, "Same Coin" (the planned split-screen dual-mindset text adventure), is currently an empty stub (`cs_tsubaki_01.rpy` is three lines). Given how load-bearing her interiority is, that's the highest-value unwritten archive piece. The roadmap's planned format (managing focus across two mindsets telling one story) is also thematically perfect for someone whose self is being split between who she is and who Sam's attention demands she be.

## Monika: Present-Tense Value Confirmed as the Whole Point

The roadmap is blunt: Monika "gradually falls into obscurity," is "very minor" in Act 2, "disappears entirely" in the Intermission and Act 3, and dies (implied) in Act 3, with cause revealed in *Temperamental*. My Part 1 read — that her fading will only hurt if she's first a genuine center of gravity, and that the May 24-25 material is the right direction — is exactly the design. The `dev_monika_disbands_club` scene (Feb 24, 2026) plays the disappearance as an administrative decision ("Clubs end. People graduate. No one's really at fault"), which is bleak precisely because it's so undramatic — and it only stings if the player has banked enough warm club memories first.

Her character story, "Lies and Poems," is the sharpest confirmation of the meta-layer: it's a vanilla-DDLC-format club day where **MC simply does not exist** — the narrative hard-cuts across the frames where he'd be, "like frames removed from film." The poems are about exactly this absence ("the paper / remembered being touched / but not what was written"; Sayori's "Half-Step" about slowing to match "a stride that isn't there"). This is doing real thematic work about presence, memory, and the person who isn't there. It's also lower spoiler-risk than "Purgatorium" because it enriches the meta-layer without exposing the Act 1 social plot — so it's a safer early unlock. The takeaway reinforces my Part 1 priority #9: keep showing real club literature (the May 24 poem exchange is the model), because the club has to be a real place before it can be a loss.

## Mom and the Quiet-Scene Capability

## Archive Unlock Timing Is a Real Design Lever

The archive is a second narration channel, and the stories are diagnostic, not decorative. Treating unlock timing as a craft decision matters:

- **"Lies and Poems" (Monika):** lower-risk meta enrichment; safer early unlock.
- **Matsuda's stories ("Dead Fantasy," "Ontological Seer"):** show her as creator/GM/system-maker, deepening without simply explaining. Lower social-plot risk.
- **Kotonoha's stories:** thematic, low plot risk.
- The current `mark_character_met` / disclaimer force-unlock is fine for development, but for release the gating should be event-driven for the spoiler-heavy entries.
